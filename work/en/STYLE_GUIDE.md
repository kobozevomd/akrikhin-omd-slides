# English version of “Akrikhin Trends · Comprehensive 2027” — translation style guide

## Audience and voice

Readers: the marketing team of a large pharmaceutical company (Akrikhin, part of Polpharma Group) — brand managers, marketing directors, digital leads. Smart, busy, not media-buying specialists. The deck is a strategy presentation from their media agency (OMD).

Voice: clear, confident, plain business English — the way a good agency strategist writes slides. Short sentences. Concrete verbs. Numbers do the talking.

- **US English** spelling (optimize, behavior, center).
- **Sentence case** for slide titles and headings (“Market 2027: five trends reshaping the consumer journey”), not Title Case.
- **ALL CAPS stays ALL CAPS** where the Russian is in caps (labels such as «ВЫВОД», «ПРИНЦИП 2027», column headers) — the caps are part of the design.
- Keep it **as short as or shorter than the Russian** (characters). Text boxes are sized for the Russian; English that runs longer will overflow. If a phrase is long in Russian because of grammar, make it tight in English.
- Slides are not prose: fragments, arrows (→), slashes (A / B) and “X = Y” formulas are fine — keep them when the Russian uses them.
- Translate the meaning, not the words. Russian bureaucratic constructions (“осуществляем проведение анализа”) → plain verbs (“we analyze”).

## Avoid (AI-sounding / agency clichés)

Do not use: leverage, unlock, harness, empower, seamless(ly), robust, holistic, synergy (except where the Russian is literally about “synergy of segments”), cutting-edge, game-changer, revolutionize, elevate, supercharge, delve, navigate the landscape, in today’s fast-paced…, it’s worth noting, plays a crucial/pivotal role, testament to, a myriad of, foster, streamline (prefer “simplify”), drive (only if natural: “what drives choice” is fine), journey (OK only for “consumer journey / CJM / search journey”).
No “not just X, but Y” constructions unless the Russian says exactly that («не только… но и» → “both… and” or “X — and Y”). No rule-of-three padding. No exclamation marks. No added adjectives the Russian doesn’t have.

## Format rules

- **Tags** `[1]…[/1] [2]…[/2]`: they mark formatting (bold, color). Keep **every tag exactly once, same numbers**, wrap the English that corresponds to that part. You may reorder tagged parts if English word order needs it. Don’t add new tags. Text outside tags is not allowed when the source has tags (put everything inside some tag).
- **Line breaks `\n`**: keep the **same number of line breaks**, placed at a natural phrase boundary in English (they control how titles and labels sit on two lines).
- Leading/trailing spaces inside tags matter (“[1]Output: [/1][2]audience hypothesis[/2]”) — keep a space between words that end up in different tags.
- **Numbers**: decimal comma → decimal point (3,44 → 3.44; +18,7 п.п. → +18.7 pp); thousands with comma (2 251 → 2,251; 1 000 → 1,000); ranges with en dash (10–20%).
- Units: п.п. → pp · г/г → YoY · млн → M (49,5 млн → 49.5M) · млрд руб. → RUB bn · тыс. / K → K · мес → mo · Q1’26 → Q1 2026 (or keep Q1’26 if space is tight).
- Months: янв → Jan, фев → Feb, мар → Mar, апр → Apr, май → May, июн → Jun, июл → Jul, авг → Aug, сен → Sep, окт → Oct, ноя → Nov, дек → Dec. «сен нач.» / «сен кон.» → “early Sep” / “late Sep”. «янв.24» → “Jan ’24”, «июл 2026» → “Jul 2026”.
- Quotes «…» → “…”.
- Gender/age labels: «М 18–24» → “M 18–24”, «Ж 18–34» → “F 18–34”, «МЖ» → “M/F”.
